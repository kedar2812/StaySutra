In July the Sahyadris are as good as they get and as difficult as they get, and
those two things are the same thing. Everything is green. Nothing is dry.
Visibility is a rumour. Here is how to ride it without it going wrong.

## What actually changes

Three things, in order of how much trouble they cause.

**Grip.** Not the rain itself — the road surface under it. Tarmac that has been
baked for three months and then wetted grows a film of oil and dust in the first
few showers of the season. The second week of the monsoon is the most slippery
week of the year, worse than the middle of it.

**Visibility.** Cloud sits on the ghats from late morning. It comes in patches,
not fronts, and it does not announce itself. You will lose the road entirely for
thirty metres and then get it back.

**Water across the road.** Small streams that do not exist in April run across
the tarmac in July. Most are two inches deep and harmless. Some are eight inches
deep with a scoured hole on the exit.

## What to carry

- **A visor you can see through, or none at all.** An anti-fog insert is worth
  more than a new helmet. Failing that, ride with the visor cracked and accept
  the water on your face.
- **Hi-vis over the top.** In cloud, you are invisible in black. This is the
  single highest-value item on the list.
- **Chain lube.** The monsoon will strip your chain in a weekend. Re-lube every
  evening, not every week.
- **Waterproof bag liners, not waterproof bags.** Nothing is waterproof after
  four hours. Line the inside with bin bags and stop worrying.
- **A spare set of dry gloves.** Wet gloves and a wet lever is how people lose
  feeling in two fingers by lunchtime.

## When to turn back

There is no prize for finishing. Turn back when:

- You cannot see the far side of a bend you are entering
- A water crossing is moving fast enough to push the front wheel
- It has been raining hard for more than an hour on a road with a valley drop and
  no barrier
- You are cold enough that your hands are slow

The last one is the one people ignore, and it is the one that causes the crashes.

## Where to be

Karjat and the base villages, rather than the top of the ghat. The rivers are the
whole point in July, the roads are flat, and if the weather closes in you are not
on a mountain when it happens. Ride the climbs as day trips in the morning window
and come back down.

## The upside

Nine months of the year the Sahyadris are brown hills with a road over them. In
July they are a rainforest with waterfalls coming off the cuttings and cloud
moving through the valleys below you. It is worth the inconvenience. Just take
the inconvenience seriously.

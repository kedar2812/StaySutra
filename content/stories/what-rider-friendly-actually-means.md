Every listing site in the country will tell you a property is "good for bikers."
Almost none of them will tell you whether there is a roof over the parking.

That gap is the entire reason StaySutra exists. So here is the checklist, in
plain terms, and what each item actually means when you arrive at eleven at night
with four hundred kilometres behind you.

## Covered parking, inside the gate

Not "parking available." Not the street outside. A roof, and a gate that closes.

Two reasons. The obvious one is theft — a loaded bike parked on a public road in
a hill station over a long weekend is a target. The less obvious one is weather:
a night of monsoon rain on an unprotected bike means a wet seat, a wet tank bag
and, if you are unlucky, water where the wiring lives.

## A gate that opens when you need it

Late check-in means someone is awake, or reachable, when the ride runs long. It
also means the gate opens at four in the morning if that is when you are leaving.

Both directions matter. A property that will let you in at midnight but cannot let
you out before six has solved half the problem.

## A tap and a drain

A wash-down area is not a luxury. On the coast, salt air starts working on chrome
and chain within hours. Inland after the monsoon, you are carrying a kilogram of
red mud that will set like concrete overnight.

What you need is a tap, somewhere the water can go, and permission to make a mess.
That is all.

## Somewhere to hang wet gear

Riding kit that stays wet stays wet. A room with a fan and a hook does more for
your next day than anything else on this list, and it costs the property nothing.

## Basic tools

Not a workshop. A foot pump, chain lube, a set of spanners and an Allen key set.
The things you left at home, or the things you need because a bolt worked loose on
the ghat.

## Breakfast before first light

Most kitchens open at eight. Most rides worth doing start before six. A property
that will feed a group at half five — even if it is only eggs and tea — changes
the shape of the whole day.

## A host who rides

The most valuable item and the hardest to verify. Someone who knows which ghat is
shut, where the nearest mechanic is, and whether the river crossing is passable
this week.

You cannot put that in a filter. You can ask, and we do.

## Fuel within five kilometres

Because starting a day with a detour to find a pump is a bad way to start a day.

---

Eight items. We ask about all eight before a property goes up, and the answers
are printed on the listing — including the ones that are a no. A property with
five of the eight is still a good stay; you just get to decide that for yourself
before you book, instead of after you arrive.
